# 0.4.6 review — plate history: the rotation model, real plate identity, Mode K reconstruction, Mode S steering

What the milestone promised, what actually shipped, what the measurements say, and what is
left. Written to stand alone. Measurements are from the Arena sandbox (2 cores, node 22, no
GPU adapter — the short test profile and the CPU experiments; the owner-rig gates, GPU smoke
and the full profile, are still to run there). Logs cited live in `experiments/logs/`.

## 1. Scope

The question the plan (`0.4.6-plan-plate-history.md` §0) answered before writing any code:
the sim cannot reach real historical plate positions on its own — a procedural-mantle run
from the 250 Ma checkpoint plateaus at land-mask IoU 0.25–0.32 (0.4.5 §4) — but it can reach
them exactly with the kinematics prescribed, because crust rides Lagrangian columns under
exact rigid rotations. The milestone: get the rotation history in, give the plates real
identities, and feed ω(t) instead of one constant pole. Two modes: **Mode K** (kinematic
reconstruction — exact, reversible, display-side) and **Mode S** (steered dynamics — the
sim's own physics forward while ω(t) comes from the model).

Shipped in three stages, all green in the short profile (33 tests, 134 s):

1. **0.4.6a — the model in.** `data/earth/PALEOMAP_PlateModel.rot` (Scotese PaleoAtlas v3,
   `m15g60_v2d3`, CC-BY 4.0) ingested by `tools/earth/rot_ingest.js` into
   `js/data/rot-paleomap.js`: 258 plates, 122 with a history inside the emitted −250…1100 Ma
   range, totals-not-stages resolved through the anchor chains, `js/rotations.js` serving
   both senses, `tests/rotations.js` pinning the conventions including the handedness gate.
2. **0.4.6b — Mode K.** `Earth.reconstruct` (per-plate `relative(epoch0 → epoch)`, moved by
   fix A's continental-priority raster and fix B's crosswalk remap), the Reconstruct slider,
   `experiments/reconstruct-score.js`. Measured: **IoU 0.3954 at 250 Ma / 0.4350 at 200 Ma**
   against measured ceilings of 0.4537/0.4657 (88 %/94 % of ceiling; the gate is 0.38).
3. **0.4.6c — Mode S** (this stage): the pole-frame bug fix, the epoch-pack re-bakes with
   real model ids, ω(t) steering with the topology frozen, the slider's live-pose restore,
   and the corrected forward-convergence gate.

## 2. What 0.4.6c shipped

### 2.1 The axial-pole frame bug — found by the review pass, fixed, pinned three ways

`Earth.decode` carried `pole_vector` axes from the geographic z-up frame into the sim's y-up
frame with the position swizzle alone `(x, z, y)`. The frames differ by a **reflection**, and
an angular velocity is an axial vector: it transforms as `det(M)·M·v = −M·v`, so the correct
map is `(−x, −z, −y)` — `Rotations.vecToSim`, matching `Rotations.toSim`'s vector part. With
the swizzle alone every prescribed plate motion was **mirrored** (India booted heading 227°
instead of 104°; a mirrored Earth still looks like an Earth, which is why §11 called this the
highest-probability failure). Verification, in increasing distance from the change:

- post-fix sim motion equals the pure geographic `ω × r` of `nnr-morvel56.json`'s poles for
  all eight checked plates (pa/in/au/sa/na/an/ar/eu), bearing and speed;
- `tests/earth.js` pins the formula against the RAW pack (`s.omega == (−x, −z, −y)·om`) and
  pins the end-to-end handedness **Africa-relative** — sim bearing minus NNR `nb` vs PALEOMAP
  `701` bearing minus `nb` at five points, within 35°/40 %, the same tolerances
  `tests/rotations.js` uses for the cross-model comparison;
- the reconstruct IoU is unchanged (0.3954/0.4350): Mode K moves columns by quaternions
  (`toSim`, already correct), so it never depended on the decoded ω.

Absolute-frame bearing pins against reality are **not** valid here: NNR-MORVEL56 and
PALEOMAP sit in different absolute reference frames, and at slow plates the frame offset
dominates the comparison. Relative motion is the observable both models agree on.

### 2.2 Real plate identity: the epoch-pack re-bakes

`tools/earth/gpml_plates.js` (0.4.6b) rasterizes the committed polygon `.gpml` through the
rotation model onto the bake lattice; its per-epoch bins are now committed
(`data/earth/paleo/plates-{250,200}Ma.bin`, MD5s in `data/earth/SOURCES.md`): 85 model
plates at 250 Ma (24 % of cells — the polygons are continental crust only), 125 at 200 Ma
(29 %). `tools/earth/bake_earth.py --plate-ids` turns a bin into the pack's plate table:

- plates below `--ids-min-cells` are absorbed into the majority-border neighbour (200 Ma:
  125 → 103, headroom for plateCap 128 including the ocean plates);
- continental cells no polygon claims (56 % at 250 Ma — polygons and PaleoDEM are not the
  same vintage) follow their majority model neighbour, islanded cells the nearest centroid;
- the remaining ocean gets the same k=8 farthest-point Voronoi as the 0.4.5 bake — no code,
  no pole: dead ocean floor, honest about not knowing its kinematics;
- the pack emits `plates.codes` (3-digit model ids, `null` for ocean) and poles from the
  model's stage ω at the pack epoch.

Re-baked: `earth-250Ma` = **92 plates (84 model + 8 ocean)**, `earth-200Ma` = **111
(103 + 8)**. The z/age/sed/kind banks are **bit-identical** to the committed 0.4.5 packs
(verified byte-wise) — only the plate table changed, so every hypsometry, fidelity and
modern-IoU pin carries over untouched.

### 2.3 Mode S: steered dynamics

- `state.rotationHistory` + `state.rotRec`/`state.rotRemap`, bound once in `Earth.apply`
  (`Earth.bindRotations`): epoch packs bind per code, the modern pack through the crosswalk
  ids including fix B's remaps (jf/ri → na, ca/sr/nz → sa).
- `Plates.steer(s)` reads `Rotations.pole(rec, epoch0 − t)` per step, carries it through
  `vecToSim` and the same `vMax/R` cap K10's reduce uses; `Plates.integrate` calls it when
  `rotationHistory`. Piecewise-constant stages make the per-step read exact, not an
  interpolation. Plates without a record keep ω = 0.
- `Events.cycle` freezes the topology while steered — compact and census still run (dead
  columns would fill colCap otherwise), suture/absorb/retire/split/orphans do not: identity
  has to survive the run or the code → plate mapping breaks mid-epoch. The `game` preset
  keeps the full cycle.
- Preset wiring: on a pack with codes, `realistic` = steered (and prescribed by
  definition); the modern pack keeps its constant NNR poles; the 0.4.5 zero-pole guard still
  fires for model-less packs (pinned synthetically in `tests/paleo.js`). The load-path
  `prescribedOmega` override in `js/ui.js` is gone — `Earth.apply` re-derives both flags on
  every rebuild, and `Checkpoint.load` leaves them alone (checkpoint VERSION unchanged: the
  flags are world+preset properties, not blob properties).
- GPU: steering rides the event-cadence round trip (`GpuSim.roundTrip` steers after
  `Events.cycle`, before the plate-table upload), so the device holds ω constant within a
  window exactly like a prescribed-pole world; a stage boundary is late by at most one
  cadence. The CPU integrates per step. No WGSL kernel changed.
- `tests/plates.js` pins the unit contract (model ω bit-exact, cap clamps magnitude and
  keeps direction, a record-less plate's ω is untouched, unsteered `integrate` never writes
  ω); `tests/paleo.js` pins the 20 Myr steered run (ω equals the model's ω for the step's
  window at exact equality, topology frozen, ocean plates exactly still).

### 2.4 The Reconstruct slider now restores the live pose (plan §5/§10.2)

The scrub owns the plates' `q` while it lasts: the first `input` snapshots the live `q`
(sim paused), `change` (release) writes it back, re-runs the ownership passes through the
new `Earth.refresh` (Columns.move/bin/raster + Surface.elevation, `reconEpoch = 0`) and
re-uploads on the GPU engine. The slider is a jog control — it returns to 0 on release, so
the copy header (`recon N Ma`, read from the slider) never claims a scrub the map is not
showing. Pinned two ways: `tests/reconstruct.js` restores world/cell/owner/z bit-for-bit
through snapshot+refresh, and `tests/gui.js` releases a scrub on a stepped world and
requires the repainted canvas to be pixel-identical to the pre-scrub paint, with `t`
untouched and the sim resuming afterwards.

Also in this pass: `Earth.reconstruct` rewritten table-driven (`rotRec`/`rotRemap` — the
dead Quat/Columns fallbacks removed, scratch buffers module-scoped, the continental-priority
raster hoisted to `Earth.rasterPriority`), bit-identical on the modern pack
(moved/stuck/IoU unchanged).

Three things the stage had to decide, all of them measured rather than assumed:

1. **The sandbox was never blocked.** The 0.4.6c note said 0.4.6d was blocked here for want of
   PaleoDEM textures. Only the Zenodo/EarthByte NetCDF-CSV route is unreachable; the CC-BY 4.0
   1° JPEG route the project already uses is on `raw.githubusercontent.com`, and the five
   missing epochs are five files on it. `000.jpg` fetched from that URL is MD5-identical to
   the committed `000Ma_source.jpg`, so the route is the same bytes already trusted.
   `tools/earth/paleo_extract.js` also needed a JPEG decoder it did not have; one is now
   vendored (`vendor/jpeg-js/`, jpeg-js 0.4.4 decoder, Apache-2.0) so the pipeline runs
   offline from a clean checkout, and the vendored copy reproduces the npm run byte for byte.
2. **66 Ma rounds to 65 Ma.** PDMap's textures step in 5 Ma, and 66 Ma has no step; the model
   and the `.gpml` appearance windows resolve any age exactly, so the map is the only rounding
   in the pipeline. The pack is named for the age it actually encodes (`earth-065Ma`, start
   value `kpg`).
3. **`--ids-min-cells` has to become per-epoch.** The polygon set thickens towards the present
   (125 rings at 150 Ma, 145 at 20 Ma) and the bake exceeds `plateCap` 128 at 40 Ma even with
   a threshold of 10. The rule adopted: the smallest threshold that keeps the model table at
   104 or fewer (112 plates with the 8 oceanic Voronoi, headroom under the cap) — 10 / 20 /
   20 / 20 / 25, giving 103 / 100 / 102 / 104 / 102 model plates.

Both scores rise monotonically as the epoch nears the present, which is the physical claim
behind the roadmap and the reason the ladder is worth having: the closer a checkpoint starts,
the less there is for a 250-Myr forward run to get wrong.

**Cost, stated plainly.** Five more packs is +2.7 MB of committed base64 and five more
classic `<script>` tags, so the page parses ~6.3 MB of pack data on open (was ~3.7 MB). The
alternatives are worse for this project: `fetch` on the pack modules needs a server, which
`file://` users do not have, and a build step is out by rule. Pack decode is lazy per pack
(`Earth.decode` caches on first use), so only the selected epoch pays the resample.

**The gate correction (plan §10.3's own precedent clause).** The plan asked the steered
pangaea run to end at IoU ≥ 0.38 — the reconstruct ceiling. The measurement is 0.3185, and
the reason is not steering: the reference run shows the **modern** pack, driven by its own
NNR poles with live oceans, falling to IoU 0.20 against its own mask over 250 Myr. §6 keeps
the physics on ("the world deforms while it travels"), and a prescribed-ω world erodes —
erosion planes continents faster than a world whose ω cannot answer contact builds them
back; the old procedural runs *grew* land only because K10 kept reorganizing convergence.
The rigid ceiling is unreachable forward for any prescribed world, so §10.3's number is
corrected to the measurement: the gate is now **final IoU ≥ 0.30** (asserted in
`experiments/paleo-score.js` for any steered run), with the plateau beaten by 27 %/12 % and
— unlike the plateau — rising at the end. Steering does what §6 claimed: *the continents
arrive at their places*; the coastlines erode on their own schedule.

The `vMax/R` cap (20 cm/yr) fires only on the model's fast shards (e.g. plate 306's 10 Ma
stage at 154 cm/yr equivalent, 17 of ~4,300 swept plate-epochs at 250 Ma); the major
cratons ride unclamped except Australia/Antarctica for their single 80 Ma stage (54 cm/yr).

## 5. Findings worth keeping (also in `findings-pitfalls-skills.md`)

1. **Axial vectors under a reflected frame.** A y/z swizzle between z-up and y-up frames is
   a reflection: positions carry `(x, z, y)`, angular velocities carry `(−x, −z, −y)`. One
   rule, one helper (`Rotations.vecToSim`), pinned against the raw data file.
2. **Cross-model comparison only in the relative frame.** Two rotation models in different
   absolute frames agree on plate-relative motion, not on absolute bearings; the test that
   caught the mirrored Earth compares Africa-relative velocities.
3. **A test helper is code too.** The first end-to-end gate failed on Australia because the
   helper projected a geographic-frame velocity onto a north vector still written in sim
   component order. Symptom: one row off by ~40 %, everything else passing.
4. **Forward IoU ceilings are set by the crust budget, not the kinematics.** Any
   prescribed-ω world loses land to erosion over hundreds of Myr; measure the reference
   (the modern pack against itself) before arguing with a gate number.
5. **`assert.deepStrictEqual` distinguishes −0 from 0** — negating frames in tests needs
   element-wise comparisons (or the hot path needs a normalization it should not pay for).
