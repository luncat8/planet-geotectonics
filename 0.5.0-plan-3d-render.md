# 0.5.0 — 3D planet render plan

Implements `0.5.0-draft-3d-render.md`: a 3D view of the planet — a displaced-terrain globe
with a translucent water shell, an orbit camera, a displacement slider and a tessellation
finer than the input grid. Renderer-only: no sim change, no sim buffer write, no readback.
It rides two existing mechanisms — the 0.3.5 display sea level (`Params.sea`) and the
0.3.3 relief ramp (`Params.zRange`) — so the 3D and the 2D map show the same world. The
0.3.5 controls are now two sliders with last-active enable (no checkbox); the 3D reads
only the passive `Params.sea`, so that UI change needs no 3D work.

## 0. The technique (fast and good-looking)

Five candidates, one pick:

1. **Normal-mapped globe** — shading fakes relief; the silhouette stays a perfect circle.
   Rejected: the draft requires the elevation to *affect the planet's silhouette*.
2. **Fragment ray march** — per pixel, march the ray to `R + h(d)`. Silhouette-correct and
   mesh-free, but the cost is per-fragment (steps × fill rate) across a full map area at
   60 fps, and a "tessellation" knob would not exist. Rejected as the default; kept as a
   possible rim-refinement in the backlog.
3. **WebGPU tessellation stages** — do not exist in WebGPU 1.0. "Tessellation finer than
   the input" must be build-time subdivision anyway, which is what we want: static index
   buffer, no patch pipeline, and the same code path on SwiftShader for the smoke.
4. **CPU mesh rebuild per frame** — allocations plus a main-thread pass over up to 164k
   cells; violates `AGENTS.md` and the draft's "without rebuilding the entire planet
   mesh". Rejected outright.
5. **Chosen: dense static icosphere + radial vertex displacement from a sampleable
   height texture + fragment normals from screen-space derivatives.** The silhouette is
   the geometry (requirement), the topology is built once and never rebuilt (requirement),
   a height update is one small compute pass into a texture (no allocation, no geometry
   upload — requirement), and the lighting is three terms (the "no heavy PBR" requirement).

One rAF, 3D view on, sim playing, is exactly:

```
[sim segment kernels] → height gather (W×H dispatch) → land pass → water pass → rim pass
→ (blit to the canvas on the drained queue)
```

The gather and the three draws are appended to the play segment encoder — **once per
rAF, never per sim step** (the `renderTail` pattern the 2D world draw already uses), so a
20-steps/frame setting costs the same 3D pass as a 1-step one. The draw targets the
renderer's own texture and the canvas only ever receives the blit on a drained queue —
the 2D renderer's black-frame fix (a canvas pass queued behind a heavy segment presents
black) applies verbatim to 3D.

## 1. The mesh (tessellation, finer and smoother than the input)

A unit icosphere: the 12-vertex seed, midpoint subdivision with a dedup cache, every
midpoint normalized onto the sphere. Built in `js/render3d.js` (the grid's builder makes
the *cell* grid, a different object; this is a plain mesh).

- Counts: vertices `10·4^k + 2`, triangles `20·4^k` (Euler on the subdivided icosahedron:
  V = 2 + 20·4^k − 30·4^k). Edge arc ≈ 1.05–1.11 rad before subdivision (the seed edge is
  arccos(1/√5) ≈ 63.4°; midpoint subdivision spreads edges over that range), i.e.
  ≈ 63°/2^k: k = 6 → 40,962 v / 81,920 t / ≈ 110 km edge; k = 7 → 163,842 / 327,680 / 55 km;
  k = 8 → 655,362 / 1,310,720 / 27 km; k = 9 → 2,621,442 / 5,242,880 / 14 km.
  Cells are 223 / 112 / 56 km at L5/L6/L7, so **default k = 8 is sub-cell at every level**
  (27 km ≈ 0.49× the L7 cell). The silhouette at 1080p (planet ≈ 900 px, ≈ 2,830 px of rim)
  crosses ≈ 2π/(edge arc) ≈ 1,490 mesh edges at k = 8 — ≈ 1.9 px facets, a clean limb — while
  k = 7 would show ≈ 3.8 px facets and k = 6 ≈ 7.6 px.
- The mesh is **level-independent** (unit directions): one mesh per `k`, reused across
  all sim levels and both engines; a level switch never touches it. The `3D detail`
  select (Adjust) rebuilds it on change — a one-time JS build (≈ 0.2 s at k = 8, ≈ 1 s at
  k = 9) plus one-time buffer uploads (k = 8: 7.9 MB positions + 15.7 MB indices; k = 9:
  94 MB) — control-change class, never frame class. Range k = 6…9 (k = 6 for weak GPUs).
- Storage: positions `Float32Array` (unit xyz), indices `Uint32Array`, uploaded once per
  (device, k) with `queue.writeBuffer`.

Smoothness comes from the sampling, not the topology: every vertex displaces by the
height *at its own direction* (below), so the surface is a smooth bilinear interpolant of
the cell elevations — no dual-polygon faceting, finer than the grid, and a mountain on
the limb bulges the rim exactly as the draft requires.

## 2. The height texture and its per-frame update (no rebuild, no allocation)

- Texture: equirectangular `R32Float`, **2048 × 1024 for every k** (equator texel 19.6 km
  — finer than every offered sim level, so the mesh never aliases the content; the 2D
  map is proven at half this texel density); 8.4 MB, allocated once per 3D session.
  Clamp rows; the u seam wraps in the sampler math (below), so no duplicated seam
  column is written.
- **Gather pass** (compute, 2,097,152 threads, one per texel, in the segment encoder
  after the sim kernels — it reads the segment's final `z`, the same heights the 2D map
  shows, so the two views cannot disagree):

```
texel (x, y) → (lon, lat)            (the sourceCell convention of the 2D shader)
             → cell via LOOK          (the existing 1024×512 lookup buffer, uploaded once)
             → z (f32)                (sim cellF, or the uploaded cellZ — below)
             → textureStore(height, x, y)
```

  A gap cell (NaN `z`) stores the gap marker `−1e9`: an unmistakable pit (no real
  elevation is ±1e9); the vertex stage clamps the displacement floor at −15 km
  (`Water.ZLO`), so the marker can never drive a vertex outside the frustum — the pit
  hides under the water shell, never a hole in the surface.
- **Sampling is manual bilinear, four `textureLoad` taps** (one `heightAt(uv)` helper in
  the module): `r32float` is *unfilterable* in core WebGPU — a filtering sampler on it is
  a validation error, and the `float32-filterable` feature is not portable. Four taps in
  the vertex stage buy a smooth bilinear surface with no sampler in the bind group at
  all, exact f32 heights, and u-wrap handled by the tap indices (`x0 = −1 → W−1`,
  `x1 = W → 0`; rows clamp). The land and water fragment stages read the *same*
  `heightAt` for their colour ramps, so a coastline's colour and geometry cannot
  disagree by even one texel.
- The module is **one template with the one-line z read injected** (the
  `// layout constants appended here` replace-marker pattern `render-gpu.js` already
  uses; the markers are single-sourced as `Render3D.M_*` constants the templates
  interpolate and `gatherCode`/`renderCode` replace by, so they cannot drift) — two
  instantiations, no duplicated logic. Each injected `var<storage, read>` binding
  carries its own `@group`/`@binding` attributes; only the mode's z source is declared
  (declaring both, or injecting one unbound, is a WGSL parse error — the 3D
  black-screen class of bug; `tests/wgsl-struct.js` now rejects both shapes for every
  kernel source):
  - **GPU sim engine:** the gather binds the sim's `cellF` (`CELLF[c*8u].w`) and `LOOK`
    through the renderer's own bind group on the same device — **zero copy, zero
    readback**;
  - **CPU sim engine:** a per-frame `queue.writeBuffer` of a pre-allocated
    `Float32Array(V)` (`state.z` → f32, NaN → gap marker; 41 KB at L5, 164 KB at L6,
    655 KB at L7 — an async DMA, sub-ms class; the round-trip experiments put MB-scale
    transfers at ms-class) into a renderer-owned `cellZ` buffer; the gather's f32-source
    variant reads it.
- Pause / view-only frames: a standalone encoder (gather + the three passes) submitted on
  the drained queue, then the blit — the 2D renderer's paused `redraw` + `present`
  pattern, unchanged.
- Per-frame allocation: none. Texture, buffers, the `cellZ` scratch and the 96-byte
  uniform `ArrayBuffer` are created at 3D init or detail change; command encoders per
  pass, like every existing pass.
- **The 3D never resamples or caches height.** Every `z` it consumes is the sim's own
  segment-final elevation — the `cellF` bind (GPU) or the per-frame `cellZ` upload (CPU) —
  so the map and the shell cannot disagree. Because the height texture is one fixed size
  for every k and rebuilt each frame by the gather from whatever the sim produces, the
  mesh detail is already independent of the sim resolution: a start map baked at a new
  resolution (0.4.0) or a future live-resolution change needs zero 3D work.

## 3. Water (translucent, no waves)

- The same mesh, displaced by the **constant** `Params.sea` (the 0.3.5 display level —
  level or volume mode, automatically, live): a flat shell at the sea level, in unit
  space `dir·(1 + sea·exag/R)`.
- Fragment: a depth ramp from the seafloor height (the same `heightAt` the vertex stage
  used, so the ramp's shoreline is the geometry's shoreline), the same shore-brightening
  the 2D z branch uses, sea-relative — shelves read bright, trenches dark, through the
  shell — alpha `0.45 + 0.25·pow(1 − dot(N,V), 2)` (the rim reads as depth), and a small
  Blinn specular from the same light (a clean glint; the draft says no waves, so there is
  no animation term anywhere in the water shader).
- Blend `SRC_ALPHA / ONE_MINUS_SRC_ALPHA`, depth test on, depth write **off**, drawn
  after the land: a crisp coastline wherever terrain pokes above the shell, the ocean
  floor visible through the shell. The 3D target carries a `depth24plus` attachment
  (allocated once per session, released with it): the land writes depth, the water and
  the rim test against it. The 3D canvas keeps `alphaMode: 'opaque'` (the 2D
  canvas's contract); the water blends against what the 3D pass drew, never against the
  page.

## 4. Camera and input

- Orbit camera: yaw, pitch (clamped ±89.5°), distance 1.5–10, default 3.0 (40° fov, the
  planet fills the box height). One combined `view·proj` matrix plus the eye point are
  built in JS into the pre-allocated 96-byte uniform `ArrayBuffer` (`mat4x4 vp, vec4 eye,
  vec4 knob`) and `writeBuffer`-ed per draw — the 0.3.3 uniform mechanism: a slider or
  drag moves bytes, never a pipeline. One pre-multiplied matrix also halves the vertex
  shader's matrix math at 2.6 M vertices, and the fragment stages need the eye anyway
  (`dot(N,V)`).
- The 3D canvas: drag orbits (the existing pointer plumbing: the 4 px dead zone, no
  pole clamp), wheel zooms (on the canvas only). A camera move is a uniform write, so
  **no sim deferral on either engine** — unlike the CPU 2D re-sample (~18 ms), orbiting
  the 3D view is free even while the CPU sim steps; the 3D view is the free-pan view.
- The **column inspector works over the 3D view**: `Render3D.pick(out, px, py, w, h)`
  intersects the pointer ray with the unit planet (the camera's own basis maths, the ray
  normalized before the sphere quadratic — an unnormalized ray flips the discriminant and
  invents hits) and the hit direction maps to uv exactly like the gather maps texels
  (`u = lon/τ + ½, v = lat/π + ½`, row 0 = south, no flip — unlike the 2D screen path).
  Hover follows the pointer (the shared "Info follows pointer" checkbox), a click that is
  not an orbit drag pins the column with a fresh GPU mirror through `probeClick`; an orbit
  drag suppresses its trailing click (`orbit.suppressClick`, the pan pattern). A corner
  ray misses (the planet spans ~19.5° of the 40° frustum at dist 3) — that is the smoke's
  corner assert, and the pick must agree with it.
- The view state is not saved (like the 2D view): checkpoints and saves are untouched.
- With 3D on, the 2D canvases are hidden and the 2D world draw is skipped (the frame
  loop draws the 3D instead, and the play tail is the 3D pass); the 2D renderer stays
  alive — switching back redraws on `dirty`, the view preserved.

## 5. Engine support (the draft's "GPU only or both" question)

Decision: **both sim engines.**

- The 3D needs a WebGPU device regardless (the project's graphics API; no new
  dependency, no WebGL fallback to maintain). Two sub-cases:
  - **Sim on the GPU engine:** the 3D renders on `GpuSim`'s device and queue; the gather
    reads the sim's `cellF` directly (§2) — no extra boot, no copy.
  - **Sim on the CPU engine:** the 3D boots a **render-only** device (default limits
    suffice: the largest preset is 2.6M vertices / 5.2M triangles, 94 MB of mesh buffers
    — all under default adapter limits) and uploads `z` per frame. The sim is untouched.
- Why not GPU-sim-only: (a) the CPU engine is the calibrated always-available path
  (file://, no adapter required) — a machine without WebGPU loses only the 3D, never the
  sim; (b) a CPU-sim user on a GPU machine gets the 3D for a 41 KB upload (sub-ms class);
  (c) the renderer stays engine-agnostic — one module, two source variants, one pipeline
  set — which is also why its JS side is testable headless against the stub device, the
  way `render-gpu.js`'s readback is.
- Readback rig contract: a session whose pixels will be compared must call
  `initReadback()` first — it allocates the staging buffer `readPixels` copies into.
  Skipping it is not a graceful null: Chrome rejects the copy with
  `TypeError: Required member is undefined` (the smoke's second, cellZ session shipped
  this way once).
- No adapter at all: the toggle is disabled with the reason in the probe line; the 2D is
  unaffected.
- Engine switch while 3D is on: re-init against the new device source (one-time),
  sequenced with the existing `switching`/`busy` gates; 3D-off releases everything
  (the `GpuRenderer.release` pattern, pinned by test). **Every world rebuild rides the
  same hook**: the Resolution select, Reset world and Load swap `GpuSim`'s buffers under
  the running 3D (`rebuildWorld` → `bootEngine`), so the 3D re-inits on the new world —
  new `LOOK` upload, new `cellF` bind, resized `cellZ` — inside the same
  `whenGpuIdle`/`switching` sequencing as the 2D renderer, releasing the old session
  first (the 0.3.4 leak lesson).

## 6. Shaders (the "no heavy PBR" constraint)

One WGSL module, three pipelines (land opaque / water blend / rim additive), three thin
vertex entry points over the shared helpers:

- **VS** (all three): `pos = dir·(1 + h·exag/R)` where `h` is the bilinear texture
  height (land), the uniform `sea` (water), or the rim constant `Z_RIM` — and every `h`
  clamps below at `−15 km` (`Water.ZLO`), so the gap marker (§2) can never push a vertex
  outside the frustum. The rim rides **`Z_RIM = 15 km`, scaled by exag like everything
  else** (`1 + exag·Z_RIM/R`): a fixed 5 % shell would be pierced at exag 40 by a real
  10 km orogen (the plan's old 3.9 % bound assumed 6.2 km peaks); at the `Water.ZHI`
  bound no elevation can ever poke through the halo. The UV comes from the vertex
  direction, `(lon, lat)` in the 2D map's convention (same formulas as `sourceCell`,
  lookup row 0 = south, so vertex fetch and texture write cannot disagree). The uv is
  computed **per shader stage from that stage's own direction and never rides a varying**:
  a fragment varying `u` interpolates linearly across the texture cut (±180°), so a seam
  triangle's fragments sample u ≈ ½ — a pole-to-pole strip of wrong-hemisphere texture
  (dark ocean reads as a black line; the 0.5.0 rig-round bug). Fragments rebuild
  `uvOf(normalize(dir))`; only the vertex displacement samples per-vertex, which cannot
  tear because vertices are shared.
- **Land FS:** the 2D z-layer ramp (sea-relative, 0.3.3 `zRange`, read through the same
  `heightAt` — the same four-line formula, review-pinned against `render.js` and
  `render-gpu.js`) + Lambert diffuse from one fixed world-space light
  `normalize(0.55, 0.3, −0.78)` + a limb term `0.75 + 0.25·dot(N,V)`. Three terms; no
  IBL, no roughness, no noise, no texture beyond the height.
- **Water FS:** §3. **Rim FS:** `pow(1 − dot(N,V), 3) · skyColor`, additive, back-cull —
  ~15 lines, no state; the planet reads as a planet instead of a disc. (It is the one
  line of atmosphere; removable without touching anything else.)
- **Normals:** `normalize(cross(dFdx(worldPos), dFdy(worldPos)))`, flipped to face the
  camera — no tangent plumbing, robust to any displacement, and it reads cleanly at
  planet scale. (The alternative — a 3-tap finite difference in the VS — is noted and
  rejected: three extra fetches per vertex for a difference invisible at this scale.)
- **Draw uniform** (96 bytes, written per draw): `mat4x4 vp, vec4 eye,
  vec4(exag, sea m, zRange m, pad)`. The Displacement slider, the 0.3.5 sea and the 0.3.3
  relief range all ride it — live, no rebuild. The light is a baked const, fixed in
  world space (stable screenshots; the camera orbits under it).

## 7. Sim interaction, determinism, memory

- Renderer-only: no sim buffer write, no readback, no sim parameter. The sim ensemble is
  bit-identical (untouched), the bench is unchanged (`bench.html` does not load the 3D;
  on `index.html` the 3D is off by default, so the default page is the same page).
  **Rig find (0.5.0)**: faithful displacement exposed what the 2D colour ramp clamped
  away - single columns piled thousands of km of sediment (probed at 3768 km; headless
  hunts: 1173 km by 600 Myr). The fix lives in the sim, not the renderer: sediment counts
  as thickness in the collision barrier (plates.js, the "thick and hard" strength
  feedback) and in gravitational collapse (it flows off over-thick margins), and a column
  accepts it only while its total thickness is under the collapse regime - the excess
  underthrusts with the slab (the `subductedSed` ledger). No free constant; legacy piles
  relax in one frame.
- Memory with 3D on, k = 8: mesh 23.6 MB + height texture 8.4 MB + 3D target texture
  (2048×1024 backing, 8.4 MB) + its `depth24plus` attachment (8.4 MB) + `cellZ`
  ≤ 0.7 MB ≈ **50 MB**; k = 9 ≈ 120 MB. Released on 3D-off, world rebuild and engine
  switch — pinned by test (the 0.3.4 leak lesson).
- The perf strip gains one `3D` line (gather / land / water / rim ms, 2 Hz): the 3D owns
  a **small timestamp ring of its own** (one 8-query set, two resolve+map buffer pairs —
  the `GpuSim.ts` pattern, not its `TS_MAX` table, which stays the sim's), requested on
  the 3D device when the adapter offers `timestamp-query`, silent when it does not. No
  new readback path in the frame loop; the map happens on the 2 Hz tick.

## 8. UI and URL

- View mode fieldset: a **3D view** checkbox on top of the layer buttons. On: the 3D
  canvas takes the map slot (the 2D canvases hidden — the `map`/`mapgpu` pattern), the
  layer buttons are inert (the 3D is the elevation-and-water look; mapping the 2D layers
  into 3D colouring is backlog).
- Adjust group, after the 0.3.5 sea controls: **Displacement ×** (1–40, step 0.5,
  default 10 — a 6 km orogen reads ≈ 4 px at default, 8 px at 20×, 17 px at 40× on a
  900-px planet; the 2D's 6.5 km relief range is the visual reference) and **3D detail**
  (k: 6/7/8/9, default 8). Both apply live; detail rebuilds the mesh one-time (§1).
- Capture header (non-default only): `3d on` when the view is on, then `disp 12x` and
  `k8` only when they leave their defaults — the `adj` line's convention.
- URL pre-fill: `?v3d=1&disp=12&k3d=8` (out-of-range values ignored, as always). The 3D
  boot rides the engine boot's completion (one `maybeBootV3d` gate both paths call), so
  `?v3d=1&engine=gpu` inits the 3D on the sim device after `GpuSim.init` settles, and
  `?v3d=1` alone boots the render-only device then. The `map3d` canvas is sized in the
  HTML (2048×1024; the smoke's own canvas is 512×256) — the 3D target and its depth
  attachment take the canvas backing size, like the 2D world texture takes the lookup
  raster's.
- The displacement floor is **1×**, not 0: below 1 the terrain compresses while the water
  shell holds its own height, land and shell cross, and at 0 the terrain collapses into the
  shell. The knob's job is to exaggerate relief — there is no useful view below 1, and
  inversion is not a view.
- The probe line reports the adapter failure when the toggle is disabled, and the 3D
  session's k and footprint while it is on.

## 9. Performance budget (estimates; pinned at the rig)

- The 3D pass is once per rAF (segment tail), never per step: the 20-step setting adds
  the same one pass. At the 5/20-step settings the added GPU time is the pass itself
  (honest note: it sits inside the drained-segment boundary, so reported ms/step rises by
  the pass time per rAF — measured and shown on the strip, not hidden).
- Desktop, L5–L7, k = 8, 1080p: gather 2.1M texels ≈ 0.3–0.8 ms; the three passes ≈
  2M vertices + ~4M fragments ≈ 2–4 ms; **total ≤ ~5 ms per rAF** at 1 step (target:
  60 fps holds). At 20 steps the delta is the same one pass. The visible frame after a
  play batch is `redraw + present` on the drained queue exactly like the 2D's (the
  segment's own copy covered the in-flight camera), so a playing frame carries the pass
  twice — the same doubling the 2D world draw has always had.
- CPU engine, L5: added JS ≈ 0.3 ms (z scratch fill + `writeBuffer` + encoder); the
  device cost is the same as above. L7 CPU: the z upload is 655 KB (still sub-ms class);
  the sim there is a few frames/s anyway, and the 3D follows the sim's rate.
- SwiftShader (sandbox / smoke): slow — the smoke section uses a 512×256 canvas at
  k = 6 to stay well under a second.

Order:
1. mesh + height texture + land pass (GPU engine): Displacement slider, elevation look,
   orbit — a planet on screen is the milestone;
2. water shell + lighting + rim + the paused/view-only path;
3. the CPU-engine path (render-only device, z upload) + engine-switch re-init;
4. UI (toggle, detail, header, URL) + tests + smoke.

Acceptance:

1. **Silhouette (the draft's hard requirement):** at disp ≥ 10×, an orogen's limb bulge
   is visible at 1080p and the rim is not a perfect circle (smoke bounding box +
   owner-rig shots); oblique views show the relief in the shading, not just in colour.
2. **No rebuild, no allocation:** a 1000-frame L6 play with 3D on — the mesh, texture
   and scratch are allocated once (rig counters), the retained-memory test is green with
   3D on, and the sim is bit-identical (ensemble unchanged).
3. **Both engines:** 3D live on the CPU (L5) and the GPU (L5/L7); no adapter → disabled
   toggle with the reason; the sim is unaffected in every case.
4. **The sliders:** Displacement 1–40× applies live on both engines (default 10, floor 1
   — no terrain inversion); 3D detail k 6–9 applies live with a one-time rebuild; the
   water shell follows the 0.3.5 level/volume live.
5. **Budget:** the 3D pass is ≤ 5 ms per rAF at L5/L6/L7 on the owner rig (measured);
   60 fps holds at 1 step; the 20-step ms/step delta equals the measured pass time
   (shown on the strip).
6. **Clean, not PBR:** the fragment shaders take one texture (the height), no noise or
   animation terms (smoke shader-string check), and the water is flat — no waves.
7. **The default page is unchanged:** 3D off → no device, no canvas, no allocation (gui
   pin); the bench and the ensemble are bit-identical.

Backlog (out of scope): the 2D layers (plate, ore, damage) as 3D colouring; probe
picking on the 3D canvas (a ray–sphere → LOOK lookup would reuse `probeAt`); a
ray-marched rim refinement; a sun-follows-camera option; water waves (the draft says
no); a starfield; multiple lights.
