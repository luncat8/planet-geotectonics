# 0.8.1 review — globe cutter, map interaction and manual export

## Verdict

The globe side of 0.8.1 is complete against `0.8.1-plan-slice-cutter.md` §7.3: a user can draw,
see, clear and re-export a full great-circle section on the 2D map, and every saved or copied
artifact passes the shared `SlicePack` v2 strict decoder. The walker proves legal adjacency and
full-path coverage at L5–L7, the pack semantics are pinned by exact tests, and the short profile
plus the Python suite are green with no simulation or diagnostic side effects.

What is deliberately not enabled: `0.8.2` baking and any live channel. The interactive round trip
with `planet-tectonics-2d-slice` still waits on that repository's v2 reader acceptance; its `main`
remains the reviewed v1 baseline `fd044e19f`. Until it reports, a copied or saved pack is a file
in hand, not a verified cross-repository exchange.

One release-time step remains in-tree: `js/slice.js` still carries the `COMMIT` placeholder; the
release change replaces it with the shipping revision (plan §5.1).

## What shipped

- `js/slice.js` — pure geometry and writer: `circleThrough`, `radiusKm`, `cellKmFor`, the
  cell-run `walk`, `pack` and `report`. No DOM, no kernels, no `Diag.check`, no `Columns.climb`;
  `js/ui.js` owns the browser lifetime, GPU draining and transport, and calls
  `SlicePack.validate` before anything leaves the page.
- `js/ui.js` — the section surface: element wiring, click ownership and precedence, overlay
  painting, one capture path for completion/Copy/Save, transport with the manual clipboard
  fallback, world-change recapture, and the 3D/reconstruct gates.
- `index.html` / `style.css` — `#slice-overlay` canvas above the markers (visual-only), the
  `#section-tools` fieldset, and the script order `js/resolution.js` → `port/slice-format.js` →
  `js/slice.js` → `js/ui.js`.

## Interaction precedence (as built)

1. a pan release that travelled beyond the dead zone suppresses its click — nothing else runs;
2. an active section on the 2D map owns the click — no probe, no survey, no core log;
3. otherwise the click probes exactly as before; the 3D view never receives section clicks;
4. a coincident or antipodal second point refuses the circle, keeps the origin and explains;
5. a click after a completed cut restarts as a fresh origin;
6. the reconstruct scrub disables export and says so; the 3D view deactivates an active section
   with the reason and re-offers it on release, keeping any pending origin or line.

## Snapshot discipline

CPU captures run synchronously. A GPU capture pauses playback, drains the in-flight batch or
campaign readback through `whenGpuIdle`, downloads a fresh mirror and only then walks and seals.
Every capture takes a serial; a rebuild, engine switch, newer cut or newer export bumps it, and a
stale callback never writes for a swapped world. `sectionWorldChanged` keeps the geographic line
across a rebuild or engine switch but always recaptures the new world. Volume-mode sea levels are
re-solved at capture time when the water slider moved; provenance and license follow the world's
start (`procedural-<start>` / Earth pack name, `PALEOMAP` attribution for Earth starts).

## Review decisions that tightened the plan

### Sliver runs the checksum cannot see

The v2 `sKm` field quantizes to six significant digits; at a full circumference the finest
distinguishable span step is ~0.1 km, and a corner graze shorter than that collapsed two run
starts to one value in `seal`. The walker, not the shared codec, absorbs any run shorter than
`1.5 · 10^(floor(log10 arcKm) − 5)` into the run that follows: cells meeting at a Voronoi vertex
are pairwise ring neighbours, the merged transition stays legal, `sKm[0] = 0` survives a
first-run graze by construction, and every published span stays strictly increasing through
quantisation. The codec and its pinned hashes are untouched.

### Derived status, not one-shot strings

Deactivation reasons are recomputed by the status painter (a `v3d.on` branch) rather than written
once and clobbered by the next repaint. Likewise the section deactivates when the 3D actually
takes the map — at boot success — because a failed boot never left the 2D map.

### Drawn state versus tool state

The overlay shows a pending origin or completed line whenever one exists on the 2D map; turning
the tool off stops click capture but keeps the drawing, so the status line never describes
something invisible.

## Tests

`tests/slice.js` pins: circle construction, refusals and path-identity stability; the rotated-view
click round trip (`MapView.direction` + `Quat.rotateInv` against `MapView.project`, both clicked
cells on the walked circle); walks at L5–L7 with strictly increasing starts from zero, legal ring
adjacency, exact arc tiling, quantisation-surviving spans, the seam, the poles and the pentagon;
pack semantics against a hand-edited snapshot (owner/plate distinction, host law, potential
order, wet flags, canonical gap rows, all five boundary conversions through the directed ring
slot, signed `vt` / non-negative `vp`); repeated writes identical in identity; and a failed write
leaving the clock, RNG, diagnostic counters and every state buffer untouched.

`tests/gui.js` adds the DOM contract: ships-inert controls, section/probe/pan precedence, the
refused second point, the completed-cut status line, Copy/Save through the page's own clipboard
and blob paths, the manual fallback on refusal, the download name, pan repaints, tool-off
persistence, Clear, re-cut, the 3D deactivation with reason and the pending-origin round trip, the
level rebuild recapture at the new resolution, and the GPU capture with its drain named and an
overtaken capture proven never to write. `tests/browser-scripts.js` runs the page-order chain
(`SlicePack` → `Slice`) as classic scripts, and `tests/adj.js` loads the new script set before
`js/ui.js`.
