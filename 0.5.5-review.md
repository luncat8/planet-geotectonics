# 0.5.5 review — the heightmap lattice and the analytic normals

What §13 of the 3D plan asked for, what shipped, what the sandbox could measure, and what
only the owner rig can settle. Written to stand alone. Measurements are from the Arena
sandbox (2 cores, node 22, no WebGPU adapter) unless a log is cited; the mesh and build
numbers carry over to a real machine, the pixel and ms budgets do not.

## 1. Scope

The 0.5.0 look judgement (plan §13) named two independent causes of the synthetic read, both
fixable without touching the render passes:

1. **the mesh**: the icosphere's 12 seed vertices and subdivision seams leave a pattern — a
   regular lattice does not have one;
2. **the normals**: screen-space derivatives are flat per triangle, so every facet shades as
   a plane — a low-poly look at any k, strongest where terrain is smooth.

0.5.5 ships both, each live-selectable: `Render3D.gridMesh(cols, rows)` beside the icosphere,
and a per-vertex analytic normal source beside the derivative one. The fragment stages, the
gather, the water shell, the rim, the camera, the pick and the readback are untouched by
either — a mesh mode carries no shader risk and a normal source carries no geometry risk.

## 2. What shipped

### 2.1 The lattice (`js/render3d.js`, `Render3D.gridMesh`)

- `(cols + 1) × rows` vertices, `2 × cols × (rows − 1)` triangles; the **seam column is
  duplicated** (u = 0 and u = 1 hold the same direction) and the pole rows are single
  vertices, so exactly `2 × cols` triangles are zero-area by construction and are kept — a
  regular index buffer, discarded by the rasterizer rather than special-cased. Faces wind CCW
  outward like the icosphere's (the back-face cull needs it), positions are unit directions to
  < 1e-5, and `uvOf(latticeVertex)` returns the vertex's own lattice coordinates, which is what
  makes the two meshes interchangeable.
- Build cost, this sandbox — the lattice is 10–17× cheaper than an icosphere of the same
  class, because the icosphere's midpoint dedup cache is the expensive part:

| Mesh | Vertices | Triangles | Buffers | Build |
| --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |
| lattice 512 × 256 | 131,328 | 261,120 | 4.5 MiB | 11 ms |
| lattice 1024 × 512 (default) | 524,800 | 1,046,528 | 18.0 MiB | 35 ms |
| lattice 2048 × 1024 | 2,098,176 | 4,190,208 | 72.0 MiB | 120 ms |
| icosphere k8 (for scale) | 655,362 | 1,310,720 | 22.5 MiB | 376 ms |
| icosphere k9 | 2,621,442 | 5,242,880 | 90.0 MiB | 2015 ms |

(The plan's §13.1 table carried 261,632 for the 512 × 256 row, which contradicts its own
`2·cols·(rows−1)`; corrected to 261,120.)

### 2.2 The analytic normal source (0.5.5 §13.3)

An injected block, source-variant only, both variants exposing the same two functions:

- `vertexNormal(direction, uv)` — derivative: returns the radial direction (unused by the
  fragment); analytic: central differences of the height field one texel either way
  (`textureLoad`, straight reads like `heightAt`'s taps), scaled to **true arc length**,
  `de = τ/W · max(cos lat, 0.001)` and `dn = π/H`, turned into a height-field gradient in the
  local east/north/up frame and mapped back to world space as
  `east·n.x + (east × dir)·n.y + dir·n.z`. The `cos(lat)` floor is what keeps a pole vertex
  finite; without it the longitude difference collapses while the height difference does not.
- `surfaceNormal(wp, nrm)` — derivative: the old screen-space `cross(dpdx, dpdy)` flipped
  toward the eye; analytic: the interpolated varying, also flipped toward the eye, so the
  fragment stages' lighting contract does not change with the source.
- The sea shell and the rim keep their radial normal in **both** modes — the shell is a
  constant-radius sphere, and that is exactly what the derivative normals of a smooth sphere
  gave them, so the water and halo look cannot shift when the land's normal source changes.

Cost: 4 extra `textureLoad`s per **land vertex** (once per vertex, not once per fragment), no
extra pass, no extra binding, +12 bytes per vertex of varying. The derivative path stays the
shipped default until the rig judges the look (plan §13.3); the select and the URL exist so a
rig session can switch per run.

### 2.3 Session, UI, URL

- `Render3D.setMesh(mode, detail)` swaps only the two vertex buffers; `setNormals(source)`
  rebuilds only the shader module and the three draw pipelines. Neither re-inits the session,
  re-requests a device or touches the height texture, the target, the depth buffer, the
  uniform, the bind group, the gather or the borrowed `LOOK`/`cellF`. (0.5.0's detail select
  released the whole session and booted a new one — on the CPU engine that meant a fresh
  adapter+device per detail change. §13.4 asked for the live path; this is it.)
- The detail vocabulary lives in one table (`Render3D.DETAILS`, `DEFAULT_DETAIL`,
  `parseDetail`), used by the Adjust select (**Mesh**: Icosphere/Heightmap; **3D detail**
  repopulated per mode: `k6…k9` or `512x256 / 1024x512 / 2048x1024`; **Normals**:
  Derivative/Analytic), by the URL (`?mesh=grid&norm=analytic&k3d=1024x512`, and the bare
  `?k3d=7` the URL has always accepted), by the probe (`3D on · icosphere k7 · …` /
  `3D on · heightmap 1024x512 · …`) and by the capture header (`mesh grid`, the detail token
  when off that mode's default, `norm analytic`). An unoffered token lands on its mode's
  default, never on the first option.
- The judge-visible knobs are all live: a mesh change, a detail change and a normals change
  each apply on the running session and dirty the frame.

  the analytic source has no `dpdx` at all and the derivative source has no height taps in a
  normal.

- `node --check` on `js/render3d.js`, `js/ui.js` and the smoke page's inline script.

## 4. The previous commit, reviewed

The 0.4.6d commit was re-read and re-run before this increment; three things were found and
are fixed here:

1. **`tests/gui.js` carried two leftover `console.log('DEBUG', …)` calls** from a session that
   was chasing the 3D inspector. Deleted — a passing test must not print bookkeeping.
2. **The click half of that inspector test proved nothing.** The old sequence left the orbit's
   click suppression set from an earlier drag, so the "a click pins a 3D column too" assertion
   was satisfied by the probe text the *hover* had already painted, and the pick count never
   moved. The test now pins the three cases separately: the click a finished orbit hands back
   is not a pick (count unchanged), a click with no drag in front of it **is** a pick at its
   own pixel, and a drag-ended click is not. That is the real behaviour of `orbit.suppressClick`
   and it is now what the test claims.
3. **`Render3D.release()` leaked the readback staging buffer** (`this.staging = null` without
   `destroy`), which the smoke's `initReadback()` allocates per session. Fixed and pinned.

Nothing else in the 0.4.6d tree needed changing: the short profile was green (33 tests,
169 s), the reconstruct/paleo ladders and the Earth-start wiring were as documented, and the
0.5.0 renderer's frame contract (one encoder, gather → land → water → rim; `present()` alone
on the canvas) is untouched by this increment.

## 5. The honest gaps

- **No GPU in this sandbox.** Nothing here compiles WGSL or reads a pixel: the lattice's look,
  the analytic normals' look, and the plan's ≤ 5 ms/frame budget are owner-rig gates. The
  smoke page's §3D now covers them (see §6) — a lattice session with analytic normals must
  draw the planet centre, keep the silhouette bounded, and survive a live swap back to the
  icosphere — but the judgement that motivated §13 ("does it still read synthetic?") is a pair
  of eyes on the rig, which is exactly why both sources are selectable and the derivative one
  is still the default.
- **The lattice's pole slivers are degenerate, not exactly zero.** The seam columns differ in
  the last ulp (cos/sin at ±π), so the pole quads' discarded triangles are ~1e-17 in area
  rather than 0; their winding is undefined and the geometry test skips them (they cannot cover
  a pixel). `heightAt` mixes the same two texels for both seam vertices, so the *height* on the
  seam is exact.
- **2048 × 1024 is a k9-class setting** (72 MiB of buffers, ~96 MiB with the target, height and
  depth): priced like one, and the analytic source adds 4 taps × 2.1 M vertices per frame.
  The plan's budget carries over but is unmeasured here.
