Thirteen Earth-anchored class rows turn a quantized potential into a tonnage off a percentile ladder, grades with the published negative grade–tonnage correlation, ore bodies that reproduce the tonnage exactly, a depth interval, contained metal, and a viability verdict naming the failing leg. Every number is a function of integer buckets and a hash of (seed, class, quantized body direction), so it does not move when the sim drifts under a bucket.

**Five things the first measurement changed** — all now in the plan, not just in the code:

1. **Burial has to know its host.** One global `+min(hSed, 4 km)` buried coal, uranium, potash, placer and BIF below their own mining depth — but those classes *are* the sediment pile. Per-row `hosted` flag; uranium went 1 viable → 32, potash 1 → 12.
2. **The potentials don't share a scale.** `oPla` tops out at 0.144 against a shared `depositMin` of 0.30, so the placer half of the feature was **dead code producing zero records**, and every test passed. Fixed with a measured per-field scale.
3. **Iron needed both of design §8's settings.** The exposure rule alone matched *one cell on the whole planet*; the old-basin (Superior-type BIF) rule supplies the rest.
4. **Two table numbers contradicted their own cited sources** (uranium ladder 3× below the published roll-front average; coal "seams" 40 m thick against a row saying 0.5–8 m).
5. **The arc variant split wasn't exclusive**, so the test for it passed or failed by seed.

**The honest part.** Where the field saturates (VMS, porphyry) the median record lands on the published median. Where it tops out near 0.7, it's the *p90* record that does, and `basin/uranium` is still 5× short. That's reported in plan §10 and §4 of the review rather than tuned away — the distribution check lives in `experiments/deposit-calibration.js` with a committed log, so it stays a measurement instead of an assertion with a tolerance picked to make it pass.
