# 0.5.6 review — the continuous-height lookup (slice 1), the vertex gather (slice 2) and the page's Height control (slice 3)

## 1. Scope

`0.5.0-plan-3d-render.md` §14 splits the height-mode work into four slices. Slice 1 shipped in
commit `49aeb39` as a pure CPU module: `js/heightfield.js`, `tests/heightfield.js`,
`experiments/heightfield-calibration.js` and the log in `experiments/logs/`. This review reads
that module against §14.2 and §14.4 the way the plan states the contract, reproduces what it
suspects against the real code, and then adds slice 2 — the gather variant in `js/render3d.js`,
the device readback rig and its structural tests — and slice 3 — the page's Height control,
the row-band build behind it and the late `attachRecords` on the session. The device half of
slices 2-3 was not run here (no adapter in the sandbox); what it changes and what it must be
measured on is §5 below.

## 2. What slice 1 gets right

The plan's decision is Candidate A: locate a direction in one face of the cell-centre
triangulation and interpolate with barycentric weights there. The module does that at the
height-texture resolution, with the record layout the gather shader reads (3 x u32 cell
indices, two u16 weights in the fourth word, the third the remainder), and its CPU reference
sampler is written in the arithmetic the shader will use: the packed word unconventionally
split (see §4.3), a gap dropped from both sums and the rest renormalized, three gaps the gap
marker. The measures in the commit message reproduce: 0 fallbacks and 0 clamped weights at
L5-L7, constants and sample centres exact, shared edges agreeing to 1.4e-11 m (L5) and 7.7e-11 m
(L7), quantization 0.026/65535 of field range against a 2/65535 budget, 32 MiB and 1.2-1.4 s to
build 2048x1024, and the plateau share that motivates the mode falling from 76-94 % of texel
pairs at 2048x1024 (52 % even at the 1024x512 prototype) to 0.00 %.

Two independent checks were added rather than re-argued:

- **A full scan.** Every triangle of the L3 triangulation (1280 faces) is tested against every
  texel direction with the module's own exact predicate, and against the record. Before this
  review that check could not be written at all (§3.1); it now passes: exactly one face covers
  each of the 2048 directions, that face is the record's, and the recorded weights match an
  independent planar solve in the plane's own 2D frame to 1e-9.
- **The generated shaders compile.** `naga` (the validator behind wgpu) is installable in the
  sandbox — `naga-wasi-cli`, path rules in `findings-pitfalls-skills.md` — and all 60 sources
  the repo generates (every kernel at both scan widths, the renderer, the blit, all four 3D
  shader variants) parse, type-check and validate clean, with a negative control to prove the
  check bites. The `hex` gather's SPIR-V after the slice-2 refactor is byte-identical to the
  one shipped in `49aeb39`.

## 3. Two defects, each reproduced before it was fixed

### 3.1 The containment test accepted the antipodal face

`chooseFace` decided "the direction is inside the face" from three triple products sharing a
sign. That condition is satisfied for ±d indifferently: the triangle at the antipode negates
all three products and passes it with the same weights. At L3 64x32 every one of the 2048
texels was inside exactly two faces — its own and the face at its antipode:

```
texel 0 · dir (-0.049009, -0.998795, -0.002408)
  [16, 209, 211] (north pole)  sum -1.84e-2  w 0.63827 0.01805 0.34368  plane offset -0.996
  [36, 471, 491] (south pole)  sum +1.84e-2  w 0.63827 0.34368 0.01805  plane offset +0.996
```

No shipped output was wrong: the builder only enumerates faces at the seed and its ring, and an
antipodal face is never among them. Verified the hard way rather than argued — the fix was
reverted and the record bytes rebuilt at L3 (seeds 7 and 11) and L5 (seed 7) came out
byte-identical, so it changed no record and no calibration number. What was wrong was the
predicate itself, and it kept the strongest available verification impossible: with two
"containing" faces per direction there is no unique answer to assert the record against. `faceInside` now also requires the plane offset
`det(a, b, c)` and the barycentric sum to share a sign — both are scaled by the same ray
parameter `t = det / sum`, so the test is exactly "the intersection lies along the ray" — and
`faceInside` is exported so the predicate, not just its consequences, can be pinned. Both new
tests bite on their own: with the front test reverted, the antipode probe fails on 11 of 11
sampled faces and the L3 full scan reports every one of the 2048 directions inside two faces. The
determinant is computed only for candidates that already share a sign, so the build time did not
move (1.18-1.28 s against 1.27-1.36 s before, within run noise).

### 3.2 The calibration's seed changed nothing

`experiments/heightfield-calibration.js [levels] [seed]` printed `seed N` in its header while
the seed reached nothing: the geodesic grid's geometry is seed-independent (`Grid.build` uses
the seed for its own noise fields; positions, rings and the lookup raster do not depend on it),
and the script samples its own analytic relief. Four seeds printed byte-identical tables, which
makes a repeated run look like a robustness sweep when it is the same run. The seed now shifts
the synthetic field's phase, and the header says that it varies the sampled data, not the
triangulation — the honest description of what the flag can do.

## 4. Slices 2-3 — the vertex gather and the page wiring

### 4.1 What is in

`js/render3d.js` gains a second gather variant, cached with the first at session setup:

- the gather template is split at two new injection markers, `M_REC` (the record binding,
  declared and bound in the `vertex` variant only) and `M_TEXEL` (the per-texel height source:
  the nearest-cell read, verbatim from 0.5.0, or the record interpolation). `gatherCode` takes
  the height mode as a sixth argument and defaults to `hex`;
- `Render3D.gatherEntries(withRecords)` is the one source for the layout table, so the binding
  the shader compiles against and the entries the layout declares cannot drift;
- `init` accepts `opts.records` (a `Uint32Array` of `TW * TH * 4`, i.e. `HeightField.build` at
  `Render3D.TW x Render3D.TH`; a different length throws rather than reading out of range) and
  `opts.heightMode`, uploads the records into a buffer the session owns, and builds the second
  pipeline, layout and bind group. Nothing else allocates;
- `setHeightMode('hex' | 'vertex')` is the live swap: a state change the next frame's gather
  pass reads, with both pipelines already resident, and an unknown token or a session without
  records stays on `hex`. `append` dispatches whichever is current; `release` destroys the
  record buffer and the height readback staging and drops the cached objects;
- `initHeightReadback` / `readHeights` copy the r32float height texture back for the smoke's
  diff (2048 x 4 bytes is already 256-byte row aligned, so the copy carries no padding).

`tests/render3d.js` gains the JS contract: the wrong-size lookup is refused, both pipelines
exist and the vertex bind group binds the record buffer at 3, a vertex frame runs the vertex
pipeline (the stub now records the pipeline and bind group each pass used), the live swap
changes which pipeline a frame runs while no device object is created, a mesh or normals swap
leaves the records alone, a session without records never enters `vertex`, and `release`
destroys the records and the staging buffer while the borrowed LOOK survives. A 100-frame
vertex-mode loop allocates nothing. `tests/wgsl-struct.js` gains the source contract: the
record binding at 3, one record per texel, the mask split `q0 = low16`, `q1 = high16`, the
exact remainder, the gap renormalization in the CPU's own order, the marker set replaced, and
the `hex` variant carrying no record read at all — plus the cross-module pins against
`js/heightfield.js` (`RECORD_FLOATS`, `QUANT`, `GAP * 0.5`).

### 4.2 The record word is decoded by mask, not by `unpack2x16unorm`

The plan says the fourth word is read with `unpack2x16unorm`, whose two outputs are the weights
and whose remainder is the third. The shader instead masks the halves itself and converts the
integers to `f32`. The reason is parity with the reference the readback is diffed against:
`HeightField.sample` accumulates the integer weights (`sum += q * h; weight += q`) and divides
by their sum, so masking reproduces the same three numbers as `f32` values; `unpack2x16unorm`
divides each half by 65535, which is a rounding of its own, and `1.0 - w0 - w1` can even come
out a hair below zero when `q0 + q1 = 65535` rounds up. Masking costs two integer ops on a
value already in a register. This is a deliberate deviation from the plan's wording and is
recorded here so slice 3 does not "restore" it.

### 4.3 What the readback rig checks, and where it lives

`webgpu-smoke.html` builds the lookup on the CPU at 2048x1024, runs the vertex gather in both z
sources, copies the height texture back and diffs it texel by texel against
`HeightField.gather` over the same heights — the CPU reference is the oracle, as the plan
requires. The cellZ session's oracle is the CPU state the upload was packed from; the cellF
session's is the mirror `GpuSim.download` left in `gpu.z`, which is the same `f32` the shader
reads. The tolerance is 0.05 m on a field of ±10 km (the f64 reference and the f32 device differ
by the rounding of the inputs, not by the interpolation). The smoke also renders the same
session in `hex` and in `vertex` and measures the plateau share of both textures on the device,
so the reason the mode exists is asserted end to end and not only on the CPU.

### 4.4 The page wiring (slice 3)

`index.html` gains the Height select — `hex` **Cell samples**, `vertex` **Continuous
heightmap**, the labels §14.1 names — `js/ui.js` the state machine behind it, and
`js/heightfield.js` a resumable `build`:

- **The build resumes in row bands.** `opts.rows` stops after that many rows and returns the
  job; `opts.resume` continues it in place, so the object that comes back is the same one and a
  frame loop driving it allocates nothing per chunk; `done` and `y` say where it is. A call
  with neither option is the whole lookup and is byte-identical to any chunked sequence over
  the same grid (pinned at 256x128 in 5-row bands).
- **The page spreads the cost.** The frame loop advances the job for about 6 ms per frame while
  `vertex` is selected and the 3D view is on: the sim keeps stepping, the probe line shows the
  percentage, and the 2048x1024 build lands in a few seconds of background work instead of one
  1.3 s freeze. A `vertex` setting with the view off builds nothing; switching back to `hex`
  while a build is in flight drops it, and a finished lookup stays cached for the next switch.
- **One lookup per world, one per session.** The records name cells of a specific grid, so a
  world rebuild nulls the lookup and the next `vertex` frame starts a new one. The session
  takes its records at init (`v3dOpts`) or through `Render3D.attachRecords` when the build
  lands on a live session — one buffer upload plus the vertex pipeline and bind group, refused
  a second time — and the mode never becomes current before its pipeline exists, because
  `setHeightMode` refuses `vertex` with no records. The control, the URL, the probe and the
  capture header (`height vertex`, only off default) all read that one state.

`tests/gui.js` pins the page half against the fake session: the select ships on the cell
samples, `vertex` waits for the lookup instead of switching to a mode it cannot run, the
progress line appears while the map keeps running, the finished lookup attaches at the gather's
resolution once and the mode flips, a second switch reuses the cache (one build, one attach), a
level rebuild builds a new lookup rather than reusing the old grid's cells, `?hmod=vertex`
pre-fills and waits for the view, and the header names the mode. `tests/render3d.js` pins the
late attach (wrong size refused, one per session, the next frame runs the vertex pipeline,
release destroys it, a frame after the attach allocates nothing).

## 5. Not run here

The device half of slices 2-3 and every timing target. `webgpu-smoke.html` and
`node tests/run-all.js` are CPU and stub runs; they establish that the shaders compile (naga),
that the two pipelines and bind groups are wired, owned and swapped without allocation, that the
page's control builds, caches and attaches the lookup in the declared order, and that the CPU
reference the device will be diffed against is exact. They do not establish that a
driver executes the vertex gather, that its readback matches within tolerance, or the §14.4
gates (gather ≤ 0.5 ms, 3D passes ≤ 5 ms at L5-L7); those need the owner rig. `--full`,
`--release` and the multi-vendor checks were not run either, and the default stays `hex` until
the device numbers exist — which is slice 3's precondition, not slice 2's.
