# 0.4.6 — real plate positions through time: prescribed kinematics, exact backtracking, and the limits

Answers one question and plans the milestone it implies: **can the simulation put the real
plates at their real historical positions, and can time be run backwards?** Short version:
positions yes — but only when the kinematics come from a rotation model, and the reversible
part is the *kinematics*, not the *physics*. §0 states the verdict, §1 grounds it in the code
as it stands, §2–§4 are the data, the rotation module and the plate identity, §5–§6 are the
two modes this milestone ships, §7 is what is explicitly not possible, §8–§10 the order, the
tests and the acceptance numbers.

Continues the 0.4.x Earth line after 0.4.5 (`0.4.5-review.md` §5 named this the next
milestone) and unblocks the staged 150 → 20 Ma checkpoints in
`0.4.0-Earth-map-plan.md` §8.1.

## 0. The question, answered

**Can the sim reach real historical plate positions on its own?** No, and this is a
measurement, not a guess. A forward run from the 250 Ma checkpoint with the procedural
mantle plateaus at land-mask IoU **0.25–0.32** against the modern map (0.4.5 §4); the run is
stable and deterministic, it simply goes somewhere else. Nothing in a self-organizing mantle
reproduces the Phanerozoic plate sequence: the real history is a particular sequence of
subduction initiations, ridge captures and continent collisions, and the sim has no memory of
which ones happened. Reproducing it *without* prescribing it is an inverse problem
(assimilating mantle state from surface observables) — open research, out of scope, §7.

**Can it reach them with the kinematics prescribed?** Yes, exactly — this is what plate
reconstruction software does, and the sim's architecture is already built for it: crust lives
on Lagrangian columns carried by **exact rigid rotations** (`Columns.move` rotates each
column's body position by its plate's quaternion; `Plates.integrate` advances that quaternion
from ω). Given a plate's rotation history, its columns are where the history says, to
floating-point error. The milestone is therefore: get the history in, give the plates real
identities, and feed ω(t) instead of one constant pole.

**Is reverse-time calculation possible?** Split the question, because the two halves have
opposite answers:

| | reversible? | why |
| --- | --- | --- |
| **Plate kinematics** | **exactly** | rigid rotations form a group; the inverse is the conjugate quaternion, already in the code as `Quat.rotateInv`. Placing a column at any epoch is one multiplication, no integration, no error accumulation. Scrub 250 → 0 → 250 Ma and the columns return bit-identically. |
| **The physics** (erosion, sediment routing, isostasy, contact, events) | **no** | every one of those kernels dissipates: erosion removes mass that went to a basin and then to the mantle ledger, subduction deletes columns, ridges create new ones. Negative `dt` does not un-erode a mountain and cannot un-subduct a column — the material is gone. A reversed sim run is not a reconstruction. |

So this milestone ships **two modes**, not one:

- **Mode K — kinematic reconstruction** (§5): place the existing columns at any epoch using
  the rotation history. Exact, reversible, cheap, and it is what a "time slider through deep
  time" means. This is the honest form of "reverse time".
- **Mode S — steered dynamics** (§6): the sim's own physics runs forward while ω(t) comes
  from the history. The continents follow real paths; the surface evolves plausibly. This is
  the mode that can pass the forward-convergence gate.

Both need the same three prerequisites — a rotation model (§2–§3), real plate identity (§4),
and a per-step ω(t) instead of a constant pole — which is why they ship together.

## 1. What the code does today

Read from the source, not from memory:

- `js/sim.js` `step()` → `Events.cycle` (on cadence) → `Plates.integrate` (q += ω·dt) →
  `Columns.move/bin/raster` → `Sim.physics`. `physics` skips K10 (`Plates.forces` +
  `Plates.reduce`) when `s.prescribedOmega`, and `s.fixedOmega` skips physics entirely.
  **`Events.cycle` is *not* gated on either** — plate split/merge/retire run even on a
  prescribed world (§6 has to change that).
- `js/quat.js` holds `integrate` (ω·dt → q), `rotate` (body → world) and `rotateInv`
  (world → body, the conjugate). The inverse needed for Mode K already exists.
- `js/columns.js` `move()` re-derives every column's world position from `s.body` and its
  plate's quaternion each frame, then `climb()`s to the nearest cell. Columns never advect on
  the grid (design §concept), which is exactly why a kinematic jump is legal: a column's cell
  is recomputed from its world direction every frame.
- `js/earth.js` `apply()` sets ω from the pack's `poles` bank and sets
  `prescribedOmega = 1` only when those poles are non-zero — the 0.4.5 zero-pole guard, so
  `pangaea`/`gondwana` run as frozen-pole game worlds today.
- **Plate identity is synthetic on the historical packs.** `tools/earth/bake_earth.py
  --plates paleo` (`paleo_plates`) builds plates as 8-connected components of the continental
  mask (≥ `--min-plate` 200 raster cells, slivers absorbed into the neighbour with the most
  shared border) plus `--ocean-plates` farthest-point-sampled oceanic Voronoi plates, poles
  all zero. Measured on the committed 250 Ma pack: plate 0 holds **24,687** cells, mean
  z **+828 m**, and **all 21,456** land cells — the whole supercontinent as one plate — while
  plates 1–8 are ocean (mean z −4,211…−4,515 m, zero land cells, 1,128–11,795 cells each: the
  farthest-point Voronoi is deliberately uneven). So Pangaea is **1** continental plate +
  **8** ocean plates = the pack's `plates.count = 9`. Those nine are not Africa, South
  America, India — they are arbitrary chunks, and one of them is the entire supercontinent.
  Nothing can be steered until the plates have real identities (§4).
- The modern pack is the opposite: 25 plates from `data/earth/nnr-morvel56.json`
  (`--plates morvel25`), index *i* being the *i*-th sorted unique `parent_morvel25` code, and
  real NNR poles with `prescribedOmega = 1` under the `realistic` preset.
- `experiments/paleo-score.js` already provides the gate: land-mask IoU of a forward run
  against the modern pack's mask, sampled every 25 Myr, with finite/plate-count/speed guards.

## 2. Data prerequisite: the rotation model — measured, not assumed

One file, and it is the only external dependency of this milestone. It is committed:
`data/earth/PALEOMAP_PlateModel.rot`, 79,521 bytes, MD5 `6cc0c0e73c4f516c6069ae1c08d4f3d6`,
CC-BY 4.0 (`data/earth/License.txt`), taken by hand out of `Scotese_PaleoAtlas_v3.zip`
(Zenodo `10.251792`, MD5 `9a8d16ab2d7f070ae3e89da7835ce4d4`; EarthByte mirror
`https://www.earthbyte.org/webdav/ftp/earthbyte/Scotese_PaleoAtlas_v3.zip`). Its header names
the model `m15g60_v2d3` and the polygon set it pairs with, `ContOCeanPolyv10u_v2d3` — those
polygons are **not** committed, so this milestone buys real plate identity and real plate
speeds but not past plate boundaries (§4 works around that).

1491 rotation lines, 258 plates, times −250…1100 Ma. The line contract, as it actually is:

```
moving_plate  time_Ma   lat    lon   angle_deg  anchor_plate  !! comment
   101        245.0    62.3  -32.1     4.11        701        !! NAM  North America
```

Four conventions were the bug farm, and all four turned out different from the first reading
of the format. Each is now a measurement the ingest asserts (`tools/earth/rot_ingest.js`
prints all of them; `tests/rotations.js` re-checks them against the committed table):

1. **Totals, not stages.** Every line is the *total* reconstruction rotation at that time,
   not a stage pole: 257 of the 258 plates carry the identity rotation at 0 Ma, and the rates
   are recovered by differencing consecutive samples. The single exception is plate `198`,
   whose 0 Ma row is a genuine 83.7° offset relative to `201` — a microplate that is not where
   its neighbour is today, not a format surprise.
2. **The anchor is the last field**, and 46 plates change anchor part-way through their
   history. A plate's rotation in the world frame is its anchor chain applied to the pair
   rotation, **anchor first**: `R_abs(P, t) = R_abs(A, t) ∘ R_pair(P → A, t)`. This is not a
   style choice — re-deriving the file's own pair rotations from the absolutes agrees to
   **3.8e-6°** over 919 lines in this order and misses by more than a degree on 269 of them in
   the reverse order. Roots are `000`/`001`; `701` (Africa) hangs off `000`, so the model's
   absolute frame is Africa-rooted.
3. **The sense is reconstruction: a positive angle runs present → past.** The sim wants the
   opposite — `Columns.move` does `world = rotate(body, q)` with `body` the pack's own
   geography and sim time running *forward* in real time, so `s.q` is a *motion* rotation and
   `s.omega` has to be the stage read past → present, i.e. the conjugate of the file's angle.
   The two are not interchangeable and the error is silent, so it is pinned physically rather
   than algebraically: with the conjugate, the model puts North America on a 233° bearing
   (WSW) at 3.24 cm/yr, South America 268° at 2.15, India 1° at 4.34, Australia 19° at 6.62,
   the Pacific 301° at 10.59 — the known present-day motions, in direction *and* magnitude.
   Un-conjugated, every one of those bearings is the antipode.
4. **The model is discontinuous where a plate changes anchor.** Two rows for one plate at one
   instant describe the same instant in two frames and must agree; in this file they often do
   not. Inside 0–540 Ma there are 42 such instants, mean jump 11.5°, worst 53.4° (plate `315`
   at 458 Ma), 20 of them over 5°. The ingest reports the numbers and keeps the file's last
   reading rather than inventing a continuous path — smoothing it here would move the surprise
   into the sim. Two anchor chains are circular at 1100 Ma (`503` reads through `531`, whose
   chain returns to `503`); both are outside the emitted range and are cut to identity, and
   the 12 lines whose absolute passes through a cut are excluded from the re-derivation check.

**The ingest emits the model's full range, 0–1100 Ma, and truncating it is a trap.** An
earlier revision of this plan cut the table at 540 Ma because the PaleoDEM maps stop there;
that was wrong, and the wrongness is instructive. With the deep samples dropped, 136 of the
258 plates looked like present-day microplates with a single 0 Ma row and no history, and
South America appeared to stop at 143.8 Ma, Australia at 94, the Pacific at 84 — which read
like existence windows and implied that a 250 Ma reconstruction had no model value for them.
Every one of those plates carries a 1100 Ma sample too. The full table has **no** single-sample
plate and 257 of 258 plates reach past 540 Ma.

What the long stages actually mean is better than a coarse interpolation: where a plate's pair
rotation is *identical* at both ends of a thousand-Myr stage, the plate is welded to its
anchor and the stage is exact. Australia reads 33.82° relative to Antarctica at both 94 and
1100 Ma, South America 57.49° relative to Africa at both 143.8 and 1100 Ma, India 56.76°
relative to Madagascar at both 206 and 1100 Ma — that is the model saying "Australia was part
of Antarctica", and reconstructing across it is the model's own answer, not an approximation.
`tests/rotations.js` pins all three.

The genuinely coarse brackets are the anchor switches: NAM 175 → 306 Ma (131 Myr, and the two
rows are an anchor change from `714` to `000` at nearly the same angle, 75.55° vs 74.97°) and
EUR 255 → 425 Ma. Those, not the arithmetic, are the error floor of a 250 Ma reconstruction.

## 3. `js/rotations.js` over `js/data/rot-paleomap.js`

**Ingest** — `tools/earth/rot_ingest.js` (node, no deps, ~0.2 s):

```
node tools/earth/rot_ingest.js [--rot data/earth/PALEOMAP_PlateModel.rot]
                               [--out js/data/rot-paleomap.js] [--tmin 0] [--tmax 540]
```

The output is a classic script, not JSON: the runtime has to work from `file://` with no
fetch. It carries **absolute** rotations — the anchor chain is resolved at ingest, so the
runtime never composes a chain and never has to know the file's reference frames — as flat
`[lat, lon, angle]` triples at the model's own sample times, plus a `metadata.checks` block
holding every number quoted in §2. 30.8 KiB for 258 plates.

**Runtime API** (classic script, `module.exports` guard, one shared scratch block, no
allocation per call):

```js
Rotations.of('101') / Rotations.find('NAM')   // -> plate record, or null
Rotations.at(p, t, out, o)                    // R(t): present -> past, slerped between samples
Rotations.relative(p, t, t0, out, o)          // R(t) ∘ R(t0)^-1: what s.q holds for a pack at t0
Rotations.pole(p, t, out, o)                  // motion ω in rad/Myr, what s.omega consumes
Rotations.stage(p, t)                         // stage index holding t, or -1 if the plate has none
Rotations.has(p, t)                           // does the model speak for this plate at t (§2)
Rotations.place(p, t, lat, lon, out, o)       // where a present-day point sits at time t
Rotations.speed(p, t, lat, lon)               // cm/yr at that point
Rotations.bearing(p, t, lat, lon)             // compass degrees, 0 = north
```

`at` takes a **fraction of the bracketing stage**, exponentiated in the plate's own frame at
the younger end — never a component-wise lerp of Euler poles, which drifts off-axis between
widely spaced samples and shows up as plates creeping toward a pole, and not a slerp of the
two samples either. A slerp looks equivalent and is not: it takes the shortest arc, so a stage
that turns a plate past 180° (plate `222` turns 186.5° between 79.1 and 1100 Ma) gets
interpolated the wrong way round, and the mid-stage test fails by exactly 180°. The same
reasoning fixes `pole`'s angle extraction, which has to use the *signed* scalar part —
`atan2(s, w)`, not `atan2(s, |w|)` — or a stage over 180° folds back onto its reverse.
`pole` is that stage rotation read in the motion sense, divided by the stage duration.
The three accessors are one model, and the test proves it the way the sim consumes them:
integrating `pole` across a stage with `Quat.integrate` lands on `relative` between the same
two epochs to **3.0e-6°** over all 583 stages, and mid-stage to under 1e-2°.

**Cross-model check** (a real, falsifiable number): at *t* = 0 the model's motion should
approximate NNR-MORVEL56, which the repo already ships. The two models sit in different
absolute frames, so only *relative* motion is comparable — and comparing ω **vectors** turns
out to be the wrong instrument, because a slow plate's ω is a near-zero vector whose direction
is noise: the same comparison that reads 69° mean error in pole direction reads like this in
the surface velocity it actually implies, relative to Africa:

| plate | point | PALEOMAP | NNR-MORVEL56 |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| South America | 20°S 20°W | 3.22 cm/yr @ 261° | 3.25 cm/yr @ 261° |
| Pacific | equator 150°W | 11.59 cm/yr @ 301° | 10.38 cm/yr @ 301° |
| Australia | 20°S 120°E | 6.33 cm/yr @ 21° | 6.57 cm/yr @ 22° |
| Antarctica | 70°S 60°E | 1.70 cm/yr @ 159° | 1.69 cm/yr @ 172° |
| India | 10°N 70°E | 2.72 cm/yr @ 7° | 2.84 cm/yr @ 38° |

Bearings agree to the degree where the motion is well constrained, magnitudes to a few
percent. Two known mismatches are left out of the gate on purpose: MORVEL's Eurasia spans
PALEOMAP's Europe/Siberia/Kazakh/Sunda plates, so `eu` is not a one-to-one pair, and
PALEOMAP's North America moves relative to Africa at about twice MORVEL's rate (same
direction, 283° vs 285°) — a genuine difference between the models, not a convention error.
The gate's job is to catch a 180° flip or a 10× scale error, and it does: the conjugated model
misses every row.

ω is capped by `Params.vMax / R` exactly as `Plates.reduce` caps it, so a model stage cannot
inject a supersonic plate.

## 4. Plate identity from the model's own polygons

The blocker named in §1: the historical packs' plates are arbitrary chunks. An earlier revision
of this plan planned to *backtrack* the modern pack's ids, on the grounds that no polygon data
was available. The polygons are available — `PALEOMAP_PlatePolygons.gpml` ships with the
rotation model and is matched to it, which its header states outright ("for use with Plate
Polygons ContOCeanPolyv10u_v2d3") — so backtracking is the fallback, not the method.

`data/earth/PALEOMAP_PlatePolygons.gpml`, 5,287,072 bytes, MD5 `d782fab51dd1a5496656f212914eb427`,
CC-BY 4.0: 471 features, 503 rings, 26,936 vertices. Each ring carries the plate id it rides
(`gpml:reconstructionPlateId`, the same ids the `.rot` keys on) and a window
`[DISAPPEARA, APPEARANCE]` in Ma-ago, with −999 meaning "still there". So `(0, 0)` is a
present-day-only feature — 215 of them are modern ocean floor, which is why coverage falls from
94 % today to 24 % at 250 Ma, the ocean floor of 250 Ma being entirely subducted — `(4500, −999)`
is a craton, and `(999, 600)` is a plate extinct since 600 Ma. 240 of the 241 ids in the file
appear in the `.rot`; the 241st is `0`, meaning "no plate", and 18 of the `.rot`'s 258 plates
have no polygon, mostly microplates.

**`tools/earth/gpml_plates.js --epoch=250 [--out=data/earth/paleo/plates-250Ma.bin]`** selects
the active rings, reconstructs every vertex with `Rotations.at`, and rasterizes onto
`bake_earth.py`'s own lattice (r = 0 the southernmost row, index `r * w + c`), so the output
lines up with a pack cell for cell. Overlapping rings are real — microplates sit inside the
plates they were cut from — and the smaller ring wins, because the file order of an archive is
not a fact about the Earth. The table it emits is in the same order as the grid's indices; a
consumer reading one and then the other has no other way to line them up.

Measured, and pinned by `tests/gpml-plates.js`:

| epoch | plates | cells assigned | what the coverage means |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| 0 Ma | 223 | 93.7 % | continents *and* ocean floor |
| 100 Ma | 133 | 39.0 % | continental crust only |
| 250 Ma | 85 | 23.8 % | continental crust only |

Identity is right, which is the point: at 0 Ma the polygons put Africa on `701`, North America
on `101`, Australia on `801`, Antarctica on `802`, India on `501`, the Pacific on `901`. At
250 Ma the centroids form Pangaea — Siberia 61°N 52°E, Africa 49°S 1°W, North America 21°N
21°W, South America 21°S 21°W, Antarctica 27°S 86°E, Australia 28°S 134°E, India on the equator
at 72°E — and the sutures hold: the great-circle paths from North America to Africa, South
America to Africa and India to Antarctica are **100 %** continental, Australia to Antarctica
97 %, while the Siberia-to-Australia control, which must cross the Tethys, is 62 %. Against the
modern pack's plate ids the agreement is 46.6 %, and every large disagreement is the pack's
nearest-seed Voronoi handing open ocean to a sliver plate (`pa` vs `jf`/`mq`/`ps`, `an` vs `mq`,
`nb` vs `lw`) — the reference is the weak link, which is the argument for replacing it.

Consequences, stated honestly:

- **The PaleoDEM and the polygons are not the same vintage.** At 250 Ma the polygons cover
  15,435 cells against the committed pack's 24,687 continental cells, an overlap of 10,871
  (IoU 0.372); at 0 Ma 89 % of the pack's continental cells fall inside a polygon, and the low
  IoU there is only because the polygons also cover the ocean. Some of the gap is flooded
  continental crust the polygons do not claim and some is vintage. Plan §11 keeps this as a
  measured ceiling rather than an argument.
- **Past ocean cells get no plate from the polygons**, because past ocean floor is not in the
  file. The bake keeps its oceanic Voronoi for those cells, or leaves them unassigned; the
  land-mask IoU gate measures exactly that crust.
- The pack gains an explicit `plates.codes` array (the 25 morvel25 codes today, model ids after
  the re-bake), so the index → real-plate mapping stops being a bake-time secret.

## 5. Mode K — kinematic reconstruction (the reversible one)

`Earth.reconstruct(state, epoch)` — no `dt`, no physics:

```
for each alive column i:
    p = Rotations.of(modelId[plate[i]])
    Rotations.at(p, epoch, q, 0)                 // R(epoch): present -> past
    Quat.rotate(world[i], q, body[i])            // body -> world at that epoch
Columns.bin, Columns.raster, Surface.elevation   // the existing ownership + isostasy passes
```

Properties that make it the right tool:

- **Exact and lossless.** One group element per plate, so scrubbing costs no accuracy and
  round-trips bit-identically (test: reconstruct 250 → 0 → 250 Ma, compare `world` exactly).
- **Cheap.** V column rotations + one raster: at L5 that is ~10 k quaternion rotations,
  sub-millisecond class; the raster is the pass the frame loop already runs.
- **Reversible in the UI.** A `Reconstruct` slider (0–540 Ma, the model's range) on an Earth
  start: drag it and the planet — 2D map *and* the 0.5.0 3D view, which reads the same
  re-rastered `z` — moves through deep time along real kinematics. The slider is display-side:
  it writes no sim parameter, and leaving it returns to the live sim state (it keeps its own
  `body` snapshot, like the 2D view keeps its own view state).
- **GPU engine:** the columns live on the device, so a scrub rides one mirror round trip —
  the same path the plate-event cycle already uses (`GpuSim` mirror), not a new readback.

**The gate this unlocks — `experiments/reconstruct-score.js`:** reconstruct the *modern* pack
to 250 / 200 / 150 / 100 / 66 / 40 / 20 Ma and IoU each result against the committed
PaleoDEM pack for that epoch. This is the number that says the ingest, the handedness, the
identity mapping and the resampling are all correct, and it is measurable in the sandbox with
no GPU. Expectation: high (the maps and the model are the same family); a handedness or
anchor-chain bug shows up as a near-zero score immediately.

## 6. Mode S — steered forward dynamics (the "by simulation" path)

The sim's physics runs; the rotations do not come from K10:

- `state.rotationHistory = 1` (with a loaded model) makes `Plates.integrate` read
  `Rotations.pole(plate, s.t)` per step instead of `s.omega` being constant, and sets
  `prescribedOmega` so K10 stays off. ω still passes through `Params.vMax / R`.
- **Topology freezes while steered**: `Events.cycle` gains the same guard, so no split, merge
  or retire during a steered epoch. Identity has to survive the run or the mapping in §4
  breaks mid-epoch; the procedural `game` preset keeps the full event cycle.
- Everything else stays on: erosion, sediment (with the 0.5.0 accommodation limit), isostasy,
  contact forces, the 0.3.5 sea. The world is not a rigid playback — it deforms while it travels.
- Preset wiring: on a re-baked epoch pack, `realistic` becomes *steered* (history present ⇒
  prescribed ω(t)), `game` stays procedural. The 0.4.5 zero-pole guard remains for packs
  without a model.
- **Gate:** re-run `experiments/paleo-score.js --start=pangaea` with steering on. The 0.4.5
  plateau (0.248 → best 0.282 → 0.252) is the baseline to beat; §10 sets the pass number.

What Mode S will *not* fix, said up front: the surface history is still the sim's own, so
orogens will not be in the right places, and the coastlines erode on their own schedule. The
claim is "the continents arrive at the right places", which is what the land-mask IoU measures.

## 7. Explicitly out of scope

1. **Emergent real kinematics.** Deriving the true plate history from the sim's mantle would
   need data assimilation of mantle buoyancy/slab history — an open research problem, not a
   milestone. If it is ever attempted it is its own design.
2. **Reverse physics.** No negative `dt` anywhere; §0's table is the reason.
3. **Real past plate boundaries and ocean-floor age** (needs the `.gpml` polygon set and an
   age grid; the backtrack gives identity only).
4. **New checkpoints before the gate passes** — the staging rule in
   `0.4.0-Earth-map-plan.md` §8.1 stands.

## 11. Risks

- **Convention bugs** (handedness, stage vs total, anchor chain) — the highest-probability
  failure and the easiest to miss, because a mirrored Earth still looks like an Earth.
  Mitigation: §9's round-trip and cross-model assertions, plus the reconstruct IoU gate,
  which is a single number that cannot be faked.
- **Plate identity drift** through a long steered epoch (a plate's cells erode away, another
  absorbs them). Mitigation: topology frozen (§6) and the plate-count guard in the gate.
- **Missing plate polygons** — the rotation model is committed but its companion polygon set
  (`ContOCeanPolyv10u_v2d3`) is not, so past plate *boundaries* stay backtracked rather than
  real (§4). Consequence: the reconstruct IoU gate measures the PaleoDEM coastline carried by
  the pack, not a boundary reconstruction. Getting the polygons would tighten §10's ceiling
  and is the one further file this plan would ask for.
- **Model/map disagreement** — the PaleoDEM coastlines and the rotation model are the same
  family but not the same vintage; a small systematic offset shows up as a ceiling on the
  reconstruct IoU. Mitigation: the gate is a band, not 1.0, and the measured ceiling is
  recorded rather than argued with.
